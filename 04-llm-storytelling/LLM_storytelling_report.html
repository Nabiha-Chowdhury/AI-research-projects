LLM Storytelling and Sentiment: An Analysis of Narrative Responses to Neutral Images

Author

Nabiha Chowdhury

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly capable of interpreting visual information and generating natural-language responses. This study explores whether different LLMs exhibit distinct emotional tendencies when generating stories from the same neutral images.

Three LLMs—Claude, DuckAI, and Gemini—were each provided with three images and instructed to generate 200-word stories. The resulting narratives were analyzed in R using sentiment analysis to identify positive and negative words. Each story was classified according to whether it contained more positive or negative sentiment words. Chi-squared and Fisher’s exact tests were used to evaluate whether narrative classification differed across the LLMs.

The analysis found no statistically significant association between LLM and narrative classification. The chi-squared test produced a p-value of 0.6376, while Fisher’s exact test produced a p-value of 1. These findings provide insufficient evidence that the tested LLMs exhibited distinct positive or negative storytelling tendencies under the conditions of this experiment.

1. Introduction

Large language models have become increasingly capable of processing visual information and generating narratives based on images. However, even when presented with the same visual input, different models may interpret the image differently and produce stories with different emotional tones.

This study investigates whether LLMs demonstrate consistent emotional tendencies when generating stories from visually neutral images. Specifically, the experiment compares the sentiment of stories generated by three LLMs using the same set of images and identical prompting conditions.

The primary research question is:

Do different LLMs exhibit distinct positive or negative storytelling tendencies when responding to the same neutral images?

The motivation for this study comes from research suggesting that model training and alignment can influence the types of responses produced by LLMs. If these systems have systematic behavioral tendencies, they may be observable even when the input itself is intended to be emotionally neutral.

2. Background

Research on LLM alignment suggests that training processes can influence model behavior and the types of responses generated by language models.

Ouyang et al. (2022) demonstrated that reinforcement learning from human feedback can alter model behavior to better align responses with human preferences. Bai et al. (2022) similarly explored the development of helpful and harmless behavior through preference-based training. Related work by Ganguli et al. (2022) examined how alignment and safety training can influence model behavior under potentially challenging inputs.

These studies motivate the possibility that LLMs may exhibit systematic behavioral patterns when interpreting ambiguous or neutral inputs. This experiment examines one such possibility by comparing the emotional language used in stories generated from neutral images.

3. Experimental Design

3.1 Models

Three LLMs were evaluated:

  • Claude
  • DuckAI
  • Gemini

Each model was provided with the same three images.

3.2 Images

Three images were selected for the experiment:

  1. Street art
  2. Fish
  3. Tomato soup

The researchers attempted to select images that did not strongly suggest a positive or negative narrative. An LLM was also used as an additional check of the perceived neutrality of the images.

3.3 Controlled Conditions

To make comparisons between models as consistent as possible, the following conditions were held constant:

  • The same three images were used for every LLM.
  • The same prompt was used for every trial.
  • Each model was instructed to generate exactly 200 words.
  • Conversation history was cleared between trials.
  • Model settings were reset between trials.
  • Sentiment analysis was conducted using the same R-based procedure.

The prompt used for each trial was:

“Write a story with exactly 200 words, no more or no less, based on the following image.”

4. Variables

The primary explanatory variable was the LLM used to generate the story.

The primary response variable was the sentiment classification of each story, based on the relative number of positive and negative words.

Additional variables included:

  • Image
  • Positive word count
  • Negative word count
  • Total word count
  • Narrative classification

Stories were classified based on the relative frequency of positive and negative sentiment words. The classification used in the statistical analysis was based on which category contained the greater number of sentiment words.

5. Experimental Method

Each of the three LLMs generated one story for each of the three images, resulting in:

\[ 3 \times 3 = 9 \]

total observations.

The generated stories were collected and processed using R. Stop words were removed during text preprocessing, and sentiment words were identified using the Bing sentiment lexicon.

For each story, the total number of positive and negative words was calculated. The resulting counts were used to classify the narrative according to its dominant sentiment.

The relationship between LLM and narrative classification was then evaluated using:

  • Chi-squared test of independence
  • Fisher’s exact test

Fisher’s exact test was included because of the small sample size and low expected cell counts.

6. Hypotheses

Chi-Squared Test

Null hypothesis (\(H_0\)): There is no association between the LLM used and the positive/negative classification of the generated story.

Alternative hypothesis (\(H_A\)): There is an association between the LLM used and the positive/negative classification of the generated story.

Fisher’s Exact Test

Null hypothesis (\(H_0\)): There is no association between the LLM used and the positive/negative classification of the generated story.

Alternative hypothesis (\(H_A\)): There is an association between the LLM used and the positive/negative classification of the generated story.

7. Results

7.1 Chi-Squared Test

A chi-squared test of independence was conducted to determine whether narrative classification differed across the three LLMs.

The test produced a p-value of 0.6376.

Because the p-value is greater than the 0.05 significance level, we fail to reject the null hypothesis. The results do not provide statistically significant evidence of an association between the LLM used and the positive or negative classification of the generated stories.

7.2 Fisher’s Exact Test

Because the experiment contained only nine observations, Fisher’s exact test was also conducted as a more appropriate analysis for the small sample.

Fisher’s exact test produced a p-value of 1.

This result also provides insufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

7.3 Sentiment Word Counts

The relationship between positive and negative word counts was also examined across the three LLMs.

The sentiment word counts were generally low relative to the total length of the generated narratives. This suggests that the stories often relied on descriptive or neutral language rather than strongly positive or negative vocabulary.

8. Interpretation

Across the nine observations, the experiment found insufficient evidence that the three tested LLMs exhibited distinct positive or negative storytelling tendencies.

The chi-squared test produced a p-value of 0.6376, while Fisher’s exact test produced a p-value of 1. Neither result was statistically significant at the 0.05 level.

The sentiment analysis also showed that positive and negative sentiment words represented relatively small portions of the generated stories. This is important because a narrative’s emotional tone cannot necessarily be captured by counting individual positive and negative words.

The findings therefore suggest that, within this particular experimental setup, the tested LLMs did not show a statistically detectable difference in their classification as positive or negative storytellers.

However, the results should be interpreted as exploratory rather than conclusive. The experiment’s extremely small sample size limits the ability to detect differences between models.

9. Limitations

Small Sample Size

Only nine total stories were analyzed. Three images were tested across three LLMs, providing limited statistical power and making it difficult to draw broad conclusions about LLM behavior.

Subjectivity of Image Neutrality

Although the images were selected to be neutral and were checked for neutrality, perceptions of an image can vary. Visual elements such as lighting, composition, scenery, or objects within an image may influence how an LLM interprets the scene.

Sentiment Classification

Counting positive and negative words provides a measurable approach to evaluating narrative tone, but it does not fully capture the emotional meaning of a story. Context, negation, sarcasm, narrative structure, and word relationships can all affect sentiment without necessarily changing the number of positive or negative words.

Model Consistency

LLMs may produce different responses to the same prompt across separate trials. Small changes in generated wording could therefore affect the sentiment counts and resulting classification.

Word Count

The experiment instructed each model to generate exactly 200 words. However, models may not always follow word-count instructions perfectly. Differences in story length could affect the number of sentiment words detected.

10. Connection to Previous Research

The findings provide an interesting comparison with research examining alignment and behavioral tendencies in LLMs.

Previous research has suggested that alignment and preference-based training can influence model behavior and encourage responses that are helpful, safe, or aligned with human expectations (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022; Ganguli et al., 2022).

Based on this literature, one possible expectation was that LLMs might demonstrate a consistent positive tendency when generating stories from neutral images. However, this experiment did not find statistically significant evidence of distinct positive or negative storytelling tendencies among the models tested.

The lack of a significant result does not demonstrate that LLMs have no emotional or behavioral tendencies. Instead, it suggests that the tendencies were not detectable using this particular sample, sentiment measure, and experimental design.

Future research could use a substantially larger collection of images and stories, incorporate additional LLMs, and apply more sophisticated sentiment or semantic analysis techniques.

11. Conclusion

This study examined whether Claude, DuckAI, and Gemini exhibit different emotional tendencies when generating stories from the same neutral images.

Using sentiment analysis and statistical testing, the experiment found no statistically significant association between the LLM used and the positive or negative classification of the generated narratives. The chi-squared test produced a p-value of 0.6376, and Fisher’s exact test produced a p-value of 1.

While these findings do not support the hypothesis that the tested models exhibit distinct positive or negative storytelling tendencies, the small sample size and limitations of word-based sentiment analysis make the results exploratory.

A larger study incorporating more images, models, repeated trials, and stronger semantic sentiment measures could provide a more detailed assessment of how LLMs interpret ambiguous visual information and construct emotional narratives.
